A Universe In Motion. Explore The Forces That Shape It.

🌌Explore The Universe. Follow Your Curiosity.

Every point of light in the night sky tells a story. Stars are born inside vast clouds of gas, planets gather from the material left behind, and galaxies evolve over billions of years. Gravity quietly shapes all of it: guiding worlds into orbit, drawing matter toward stars, and bending the paths of light around the most extreme objects in nature. The universe is not a static picture hanging above us. It is a living system in motion, full of collisions, elegant patterns, sudden transformations, and unanswered questions.

Antigravity Universe is an interactive space laboratory built to make those ideas feel tangible. Instead of only reading about celestial mechanics, you can place objects into a simulated cosmos, change their properties, watch their paths evolve, and discover how a small change in speed or mass can transform an entire system. Start with a quiet planetary arrangement, experiment with multiple stars, or bring a black hole into the scene and observe the powerful pull of its gravity.

🔭Less Guessing. More Discovering.

The controls are designed to let you experiment at your own pace. Adjust the strength of gravity and the passage of simulation time. Pause the system to inspect a moment, enable trails to follow a body's journey, and use the camera to explore a scene from a wider view or a closer perspective. Create planets, stars, comets, pulsars, and black holes, then compare how their different masses and starting velocities shape the result. There is no single correct way to explore: each experiment is an invitation to ask a better question.

A simulation is a model, not a perfect substitute for the real universe. The engine uses simplified numerical mechanics and visual effects to make complex behaviour easier to explore; its dramatic colours and luminous disks are artistic representations, not scientific measurements. Use it to build intuition, test ideas, and see why stable orbits, close encounters, and gravitational capture depend on more than just where an object begins.

🕳️Inside The Gravity Well

A black hole is not a cosmic cannon. Its gravity attracts matter; it does not randomly throw nearby objects outward. Matter with enough sideways motion may orbit for a long time, while matter that loses orbital energy can spiral inward. When an object crosses the simulation's capture boundary, it is consumed rather than bounced back into space. The bright accretion disk around a black hole represents heated material outside the dark event horizon, while any jets shown by the simulator are separate visual phenomena and are not the hole itself ejecting swallowed objects.

Think of this observatory as a place to learn by doing. Set up a system, observe its motion, change one variable, and compare what happens next. Follow the trails. Try an unstable orbit. Build a system of your own. When you are ready, enter the simulation and begin exploring the forces that shape the cosmos. Your next discovery starts with one small experiment.
